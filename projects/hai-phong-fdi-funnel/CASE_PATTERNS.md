# Borrowed Case Patterns

## Rule

Yes, you can borrow analysis from successful public cases.

No, you should not pretend those cases are your own.

Use them as pattern libraries.

## Case 1: DEEP C

## What they do well

- sell clear product categories:
  land, ready-built factory/warehouse, utilities, services
- make speed-to-start explicit
- present hard operational facts:
  distance to port, airport, expressway, utilities
- support the sale with testimonials
- position the park as both operational and strategic

## What to borrow

- productized offer structure
- location + infrastructure proof
- operational metrics in every listing
- testimonial architecture
- eco / sustainability layer where relevant

## Why this matters to you

You also need to stop presenting opportunities like vague property ads.
Each asset should look like an investment-ready operating option.

## Case 2: VSIP

## What they do well

- sell one-stop service, not just industrial land
- make investor support part of the core promise
- present pre-licensing and post-licensing support clearly
- show that the park reduces friction, not just offers space

## What to borrow

- one-stop-service framing
- investor journey support
- administrative support as part of the offer
- ecosystem framing:
  industrial + urban + service

## Why this matters to you

Your offline advantage is not just supply access.
It is your ability to move the investor through uncertainty faster.

## Case 3: HEZA / Hai Phong Promotion

## What they do well

- use ecosystem proof:
  FDI scale, infrastructure, incentives, logistics, labor, policy
- connect investor stories to cluster logic
- promote strategic sectors and strategic geographies

## What to borrow

- city-level proof points
- policy and infrastructure proof
- cluster-based market stories
- "why Hai Phong now" framing

## Why this matters to you

Your story should not begin with "we have listings."
It should begin with "Hai Phong is becoming one of the clearest industrial entry points in North Vietnam, and we can get you in faster and with less risk."

## Case 4: Pegatron / Supporting-Industry Attraction

## What the public pattern shows

- anchor investors attract supplier ecosystems
- city and industrial-zone operators actively court those supplier chains
- cluster-following logic is real, not theoretical

## What to borrow

- supplier-following acquisition
- anchor-ecosystem targeting
- account list building around existing large investors

## Practical Translation For You

Turn these cases into your own operating model:

### 1. Productize like DEEP C

- land
- ready-built factory
- utilities / technical facts
- support services

### 2. Sell support like VSIP

- shortlist
- site visit
- negotiation
- administrative coordination

### 3. Tell the city story like HEZA

- growth
- port access
- industrial expansion
- policy support
- labor and logistics logic

### 4. Hunt demand like Pegatron ecosystem promotion

- go after suppliers
- not just random foreign manufacturers
